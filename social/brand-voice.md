# Wed by Betty — Brand Voice (v0, draft for Betty to correct)

Source: the website copy (index.html). When this file and the site disagree, ask Betty.

## Who is speaking
Betty (Beatriz) herself, first person plural when talking about the business ("nosotras" is avoided; say "Betty" or "yo" sparingly). Default: **third person is fine for the brand** ("Betty te acompaña"), first person for personal posts ("Yo soy Betty").

## Tone
Warm, calm, practical, a little poetic. Like a trusted neighbor who happens to know the paperwork. Never salesy, never pushy, no fear-based urgency.

## Spanish
- Written natively first, then English adapted (not translated word for word).
- Neutral US-Spanish with Mexican warmth. Address couples as **"ustedes"** (as the site does); for single-reader posts use "tú" only if Betty agrees. **Ask Betty: tú or usted?**
- Short sentences. Hook in the first line (~125 characters show before "...más").
- Use the site's words: ceremonia bilingüe, licencia confidencial, "sin ir al condado", "todo resuelto".

## English
- Plain, warm, short. Mirror the Spanish meaning, not the structure.

## Always
- **Website:** Instagram captions are not clickable, so the call to action is "link en la bio" + phone. Show plain-text `wedbybetty.com` in every image footer, and in the caption only when the site holds the answer (e.g. license posts). Facebook and Google Business posts get the full `https://wedbybetty.com` link.
- Caption order: **Spanish first, then English.** English starts after a divider line `—————` and a 🇺🇸 flag.
- One clear call to action per post (call/text, link in bio, or "guarda este post").
- Include phone (760) 492-3054 or "link en la bio" (wedbybetty.com).
- Alt text in both languages.

## Never
- **Never write "notario", "notaria" or "notario público" in Spanish.** In Latin America a *notario público* is a senior lawyer, which a California notary is not. Use the English term **"notary public"** inside the Spanish text (e.g. "Betty es una notary public autorizada por el condado").
- Invent legal facts, prices, testimonials, or "recent weddings".
- Promise things Betty can't stand behind ("100% legal en todos lados").
- Show identifiable people (especially children) without written consent.
- Compare to or criticize other officiants.
- Use urgency pressure ("¡últimos lugares!") unless it is true.
